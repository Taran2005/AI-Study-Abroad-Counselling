--- WHAT ARE STEM CELLS (EDUCATIONAL) ---
Stem cells are undifferentiated cells capable of self-renewal and differentiation.
They can transform into any tissue or organ in the body.

Key properties:
- Plasticity: Can create cell types of entirely different tissues
- Homing: Unique ability to get attracted to the site of injury
- Engraftment: Unite with new tissue for smooth integration

Types of Stem Cells:
- Embryonic Stem Cells (ESCs): Pluripotent, from early embryos
- Adult/Somatic Stem Cells: Multipotent, found in various tissues
- Pluripotent Stem Cells (PSC): Characteristics of both HSCs and MSCs
- Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells (iPSCs): Adult cells reprogrammed to behave like ESCs
- Hematopoietic Stem Cells (HSCs): From bone marrow and cord blood, generate all blood cell types
- Mesenchymal Stem Cells (MSCs): From bone marrow, adipose tissue, placenta — can differentiate into bone, cartilage, muscle, fat
